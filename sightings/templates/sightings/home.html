    Tick identification and bite prevention

    Identifying ticks

    Ticks are small arachnids with eight legs as adults. In the UK, the sheep tick (Ixodes ricinus) is widespread; the hedgehog tick (Ixodes hexagonus) and ornate cow tick (Dermacentor reticulatus) also occur. Size and appearance vary by life stage and whether a tick has fed, so a photo or app cannot reliably confirm a species. Ask a veterinary professional about a tick found on a pet.

    Reducing tick bites

    • Use a veterinary-recommended tick preventive for pets and follow its label.
    • After walks, check people, pets, and clothing, especially around the head, neck, and skin folds.
    • Remove an attached tick promptly with a proper tick-removal tool; avoid squeezing or burning it.
    • Contact a healthcare or veterinary professional if you are concerned after a bite.
    • If a tick bite is followed by a rash, fever, joint pain, or flu-like symptoms, contact a healthcare professional promptly and ask about possible Lyme disease risk.

    Tick sightings are community reports and do not indicate confirmed disease risk.
